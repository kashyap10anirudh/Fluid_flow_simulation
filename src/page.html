Drag the round handles on the selected port to reshape it. Drag the port centre to move it. Arrow keys nudge it 1 mm (Shift: 5 mm). The sealed cold-side ports on the left copy the shape of the port beside them.

Plate pattern

Turn the distribution area off and the chevrons run right up to the port. Low chevron angles give gentle channels with a small pressure drop. High angles give strong turbulence, more heat transfer and a large pressure drop. You can also drag the angle handle on the plate.

Flow & heat

In a plate pack every second gap carries the hot water, and the port feeds all of those channels at once. This page simulates one of them, so it gets the port flow divided by this number.

Gasket line

The gasket seals the channel, so water flows only inside it. With editing on, drag its handles to change how it wraps around the ports.

View

Pin a design as a reference, change something, and every readout and the A–A profile show the comparison.

Model: 2-D incompressible flow averaged across the channel gap, on 2 mm cells. Flat areas use Hele-Shaw wall friction (12μ/h²). Chevron areas use Martin's friction and Nusselt correlations (VDI Heat Atlas), set by the local flow angle to the ribs. The distribution areas carry an open "chocolate" stud pattern with low resistance that is the same in every direction, modelled with the same correlations at an effective 25° (assumed). The plate is shown stacked against an identical plate turned over (dashed ribs); the dots are where their ridges touch. In the ribbed area, particles travel in two layers that follow each plate's furrows. They flow round the contact points, churn in the recirculation behind them, and switch furrows there: rarely below about 45°, often above 60°, where the flow zigzags. Turbulence is estimated from the energy the ribs take out of the flow beyond plain wall friction, switching on between Re ≈ 60 and 600 (calibrated, not measured). The cold side is held at a constant temperature, with the same film coefficient as the hot side.
